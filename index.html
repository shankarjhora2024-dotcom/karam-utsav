Cultural Pillars

Essential Facets of Karam Parav

From agricultural blessings to nightlong choral ballads, Karam Utsav unites generations in joyous celebration.

Worship of Nature

Two sacred Karam boughs (Daal) are carefully harvested from the forest, brought to the village square (Akhra), and established with rituals to bless livestock, crops, and families.

The Jawa Awakening

Maidens (Karamthin) plant and nurture five to seven types of grains in baskets for 7 to 9 days, singing traditional awakening melodies so the seedlings sprout healthy and golden.

Jhumur Songs & Dance

Women and men join hands in curving lines, swaying to the resonant, thundering beats of the clay Mandar drum and the sweet melodies of bamboo flutes under the full moon.

Heritage & Folklore

The Timeless Tale of Karma and Dharma

At the philosophical heart of Karam Utsav is the legendary story of two brothers, Karma and Dharma. The elder brother, Karma, was hardworking and devoted to his agriculture and elders, while Dharma learned through hardship the paramount importance of respecting nature and traditional duties.

Today in Assam, the festival stands as a symbol of unity, mutual cooperation, and ecological harmony, celebrated across thousands of tea gardens, towns, and villages with deep cultural pride.

  • Celebrated in the lunar month of Bhado (August–September)
  • Preserved orally through Karam Katha folk story recitals
  • Promotes environmental protection and community bonding
Ceremonial Phases

Sacred Flow of Karam Puja

A structured progression of communal devotion and agricultural worship.

01

Jawa Jagaran

7 to 9 days before the puja, girls place grain seeds in decorated bamboo baskets with river sand, singing Tusu and Karam songs each evening.

02

Daal Katna

Young men fast and proceed in a procession to the forest grove with drums, reverently cutting two sacred branches of the Karam tree.

03

Akhra Sthapana

The Karam branches are ceremonially planted in the center of the village courtyard (Akhra), consecrated with vermilion, milk, and flowers by the elder priest (Pahan).

04

Visarjan & Feast

After a night of Jhumur dancing, the branches are respectfully carried at dawn and immersed in the local river with prayers for harvest prosperity.
